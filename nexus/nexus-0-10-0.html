Nexus 0.10.0: searchlights that work together

by Luca De Rosa · 10 November 2026 · 2 min read

Nexus is the little tower-defence game I’m building on the side: a starship crashed on a medieval world, a maze of forest all around it, and monsters that are only ever born where you can’t see.

Most of this update came straight out of one afternoon of playing and grumbling: things that moved when they shouldn’t, things that didn’t show when they should, and one tower upgrade that was quietly wasting itself.

Crossfire

Ship’s Searchlight freezes anything its beam sweeps over. Put two searchlight towers next to each other, though, and their beams could end up lying right on top of each other, sweeping the same ground. Two upgrades, one beam’s worth of use.

Now searchlights near each other turn opposite ways, so their beams sweep through each other instead of along each other. Where the beams of two towers overlap, that patch burns: anything caught in it takes damage, and it gets worse with every extra beam. The overlap shows red where two beams meet and violet where three or more pile in. Only the overlap changes colour, because only the overlap hurts.

The cores switch

Core Rounds lets a tower fire Monster Cores once it runs out of arrows. Now, once you have Core Rounds, a tower’s panel gets a switch: flip it and that tower fires cores first and goes back to arrows when your cores run out. Good for the one tower holding a choke point.

Things that look right now

  • The resource bar at the top no longer jumps around every time a number changes. The wood countdown hangs just under the wood block instead of shoving everything sideways.
  • The Transmuter’s panel was one long run-on line. Now the recipe is big at the top, then what it has made and its hull, and the tuning offer sits in its own box with how much more wood it brings.
  • Some ground was invisible: when a silver seam collapses into stone, its mountains didn’t show up, and neither did trees grown by the Forester or freshly grown crystal. They all draw properly now.
  • The felling bar stopped about halfway with Sharp Axes and the tree fell anyway. It now fills all the way, so you can see each tree finish.

Smaller things

  • Pointing at an open iron mine’s wheel describes the mine instead of saying “open ground”.
  • Pressing C to save a note no longer types a C into the note box, and notes can be as long as you like.
